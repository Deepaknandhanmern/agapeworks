A growing share of searches now end on an AI-generated answer — in Google's AI Overviews,
Bing Copilot, or a direct question to ChatGPT or Perplexity — with no click to a website at all.
That's Answer Engine Optimization (AEO): making sure your content is the one those systems quote,
even when the person never lands on your page.

Ranking #1 for a keyword and being *the answer* an AI cites are related but different goals, and
the second one rewards different things.

## Structure content as answers, not narratives

Traditional SEO writing builds up to a point. AEO rewards getting there first: a direct answer in
the opening sentence or two, then supporting detail. Answer engines extract concise, self-contained
statements — a paragraph that requires reading three prior paragraphs for context is much less
likely to get pulled out and quoted.

## Structured data is no longer optional

`FAQPage`, `HowTo`, and `Article` schema give AI crawlers an explicit, unambiguous signal about
what your content answers. This isn't a minor technical nice-to-have anymore — it's one of the
clearest ways to tell an answer engine "here is a direct answer to this exact question," in a
format it doesn't have to guess at.

## Entities matter more than keywords

Answer engines reason about *entities* — your company, your product, the concepts you're
associated with — not just keyword strings. Consistent naming, a clear `Organization` schema, and
being cited accurately elsewhere on the web (directories, press, partner sites) all feed into
whether an answer engine treats you as an authoritative source on your topic, independent of any
single page's keyword density.

## The metric changes too

Page views from an answer-engine citation may not happen at all — the value shows up as brand
recognition, direct traffic, and people searching your name after seeing it cited. Tracking this
well is still an evolving practice, but the businesses paying attention to it now are the ones
who'll have the data to act on it as it matures.

## What we actually do differently

For an AEO-focused piece, we structure the page to open with a plain-language answer to the
question in its title, back it with `FAQPage`/`Article` schema, and keep entity naming (company,
product, key terms) consistent across the page and the site's broader schema — the same
discipline as good technical SEO, aimed at a different reader: a model summarizing the page rather
than a person scanning it.
